## The one-paragraph thesis

You are not going to win a Director-of-AI role by competing with ML researchers on model
building. You will win it by owning the intersection almost nobody else occupies:
**AI that runs on real-time, safety-critical, physically-deployed distributed systems at
fleet scale.** You already have 22 years of the hard half — edge/embedded constraint
engineering, vehicle-to-cloud platforms at 15M units, Kafka/K8s/observability substrate,
simulation-first validation, and automotive regulatory literacy (ISO 21434, UNECE R155/R156).
What you are missing is the *2023–2026 AI stack* — transformers, inference economics,
agentic systems, evals, and AI governance — plus public evidence that you own it. This
roadmap closes that gap in **12 months to credible, 18 months to competitive**, without
abandoning the moat that makes you rare.

**Positioning line to grow into:**
> *"I lead AI platforms where the output moves physical things — and can't be wrong."*

1. **Public evidence.** At Director+ the market does not check your certificates. It checks
   whether it has seen your thinking. Write, speak, ship in the open. Budget for this like
   it's a deliverable, because it is.
2. **Inference economics fluency.** The single most-demanded, least-supplied Director skill
   in 2026 is knowing *what AI costs and why* at the unit level. You are already running a
   cost-optimization program backed at SVP/CTO — that is a gift. Deepen it until you can
   teach it.
3. **Governance as a differentiator, not a chore.** You have functional-safety and automotive
   cybersecurity literacy. Almost no AI director does. As the EU AI Act high-risk regime bites,
   that combination becomes a hiring criterion rather than a nice-to-have.
